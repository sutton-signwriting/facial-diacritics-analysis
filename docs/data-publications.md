# Peer-reviewed data and resource publication options

Checked against publisher sources on 4 November 2026. These are venue-fit
assessments, not predictions of acceptance. A separate data/resource paper is currently deferred: the active outcome is the Zenodo release of the existing exploratory resource. No journal has been contacted.

## Closest short-data-paper fit: Journal of Open Humanities Data

The journal accepts peer-reviewed Data Papers of 1,000–1,500 words, including
references, describing humanities research objects with reuse potential.
Linguistics is explicitly within scope. A public repository deposit with a
persistent identifier should precede submission; Zenodo is among its recommended
repositories. Its scope distinguishes resource description from an article
presenting empirical research claims.

This is a good match for a concise account of the extracted facial and gaze
records, provenance, independent selections, validation, limitations, and reuse.
The Unicode preservation argument can remain in the separate journal article.

Gold open access has a publication charge; a waiver or discount should be
requested in the cover letter at submission. The current amount was not
established from the inspected pages and should be checked before choosing.

Primary sources:
[author guidelines](https://openhumanitiesdata.metajnl.com/about/submissions),
[scope, access policy, and repositories](https://openhumanitiesdata.metajnl.com/about).

## Longer alternative: Language Resources and Evaluation

The journal covers creation, annotation, use, and evaluation of machine-readable
language resources and associated tools. Its guidelines offer Project Notes,
typically 8–10 pages, for interim resource development, software, standards,
and smaller resources. Full papers describing a substantial resource normally
require more extensive creation/evaluation detail and comparison with related
resources, typically in 18–25 pages.

A Project Note is the more proportionate starting point for this specialized
resource. A full paper would need a stronger resource evaluation and account of
use beyond the browser's existence. The journal is hybrid; open-access costs and
available arrangements need checking if CC BY article publication is desired.

Primary sources:
[aims and scope](https://link.springer.com/journal/10579/aims-and-scope),
[paper types and instructions](https://link.springer.com/journal/10579/submission-guidelines).

## Broader alternative: Data in Brief

The publisher describes short, peer-reviewed data articles across disciplines,
including preliminary and proof-of-concept work. This is a broader readership
than the two options above. Its current scope excludes descriptions already
published as supplementary material in an original research article. Sequence
the resource paper deliberately and distinguish it from the main article's
supplement. The full author guide returned HTTP 403 during this check; detailed
eligibility for this source-derived resource and current charges remain to be
verified before recommending a submission.

Primary source: [publisher scope](https://journalinsights.elsevier.com/journals/2352-3409/downloads).

## What the data paper would contribute

A working title is **A source-linked dataset and browser for facial composition
in SignWriting**. Describe the reusable resource rather than repeat the argument
of *Standardizing the Record*:

1. Resource purpose and source snapshot.
2. Regex extraction, source witnesses, provenance, exclusions, and independent
   primary/eye-gaze selections.
3. Facial normalization and the deliberately bounded Unicode mapping.
4. Quality checks, known invalid keys, unassessed composition equivalence,
   multiple-face grouping limits, and environment dependence.
5. Data dictionary, rights/attribution, version DOI, and practical reuse.

Source-context counts and automated checks support a resource description.
They are not a linguistic validation of every face or a rendering success rate.
Disclose the companion article and shared data, cite the exact deposited release,
and follow each journal's overlap policy. A Zenodo dataset deposit and a
peer-reviewed paper are complementary outputs with separate identifiers.
